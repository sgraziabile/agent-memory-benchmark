# Calibration record — duty pump, third riser

## Asset

The duty pump on the third riser is asset PMP-310. It carries the base load for the riser and runs
continuously between the spring and autumn changeover; the standby pumps only start when it is isolated.
The riser is fed from the north header and returns through the shared manifold, which is why the
third-riser pumps are serviced as a set rather than one at a time.

## Recorded limit

The recorded limit for PMP-310 is HLX-2C7V-9043. It was re-measured during the January 2026 cycle and
confirmed at that value; the cycle itself is described in the pack index and is not repeated here.

## Method

The limit is read at steady flow with the other pumps on the riser isolated, using the calibrated gauge on
the riser outlet rather than the gauge on the pump body. Two readings are taken ten minutes apart and both
must fall inside the stated tolerance before the value is entered in the schedule; a single reading is
recorded as provisional and replaced at the next cycle.

Where the two readings disagree by more than the tolerance, the asset is scheduled for a third reading
rather than averaging the pair. Averaging hides which of the two is wrong, and a limit that is wrong in the
middle is harder to notice at the next cycle than one that is wrong at the edge.

## Follow-up

The riser manifold remains on the watch list. It has been flagged twice for the same reason: the isolation
valves do not seat fully, so servicing one pump bleeds pressure from its neighbours. The valves are on the
maintenance backlog with an owner and a date, and until they are replaced the third-riser pumps are
scheduled as a set rather than individually.

## Note on scope

This record covers the pump as an asset. It does not cover the motor, which has its own record, and it does
not cover the loop served by the riser, whose calibration is recorded separately because it is measured on
a different cadence. Where a ticket needs the value for a pump, it quotes this record by identifier rather
than by position, because position alone does not distinguish the duty pump from the units beside it.
