# Change-control checklist — platform facilities

Applies to every change raised against the facility estate, including work carried out by vendors under
a framework agreement. The checklist is filled in by the change raiser and countersigned by the shift
supervisor before the ticket leaves draft.

## Before the change

- [ ] A change record exists with a single named owner, a start and end window, and a rollback step written
      by someone other than the person who will execute it.
- [ ] The rollback step names the exact state to restore and the person who verifies it afterwards.
- [ ] Any safety interlock that must be bypassed is listed, together with the mitigation that replaces it
      while the bypass is active.
- [ ] The affected room and rack are named. "Facilities" is not an acceptable location.
- [ ] A second engineer has read the plan and signed it. For changes that touch power distribution the
      reviewer must be from the electrical team.

## During the change

The executing engineer narrates each step into the ticket as it completes, rather than reconstructing the
sequence afterwards. Where a step deviates from the plan, the deviation is recorded before the next step
begins, together with the reason and the name of whoever approved it. A change that stalls for more than
thirty minutes is either resumed with an updated plan or rolled back; it is never left half-applied while
the engineer attends to something else.

Communication runs on the change channel. Anyone who needs to know what is happening reads that channel;
nobody is expected to chase updates by message. If the change affects customer-visible service, the
communications owner posts a holding note before the first step rather than after the first symptom.

## After the change

The completion note records the finish time, the state of every interlock, and any follow-up work with an
owner and a date. Evidence — screenshots, command output, photographs of the physical state — is attached
to the ticket, not kept in a personal folder. Tickets without evidence are reopened at the weekly review.

The weekly review samples one in five completed changes and checks that the plan matches what the evidence
shows. Findings are grouped into two buckets: planning defects, where the rollback step was wrong or
missing, and execution defects, where the plan was right and the work diverged from it. The distinction
matters because the two are fixed by different people. Planning defects go back to the team that wrote the
plan; execution defects are handled by the shift supervisor with the engineer concerned.

## Standing exclusions

Routine restarts performed by monitoring automation are outside this process, but the automation must log
the same fields as a manual change so that the weekly sample can include them. Emergency isolation of a
faulty circuit is exempt from prior approval and is recorded retrospectively within one business day.
