# Air-handling inspection — rows 3 and 5

## What was inspected

Two air-handling units were inspected in the same visit: AHU-207, which serves row 3 of the north hall, and
AHU-233, which serves row 5. They were inspected together because they share a filter bank access corridor
and separating them would have meant erecting the same scaffold twice.

## Results

Both units passed. AHU-207 recorded an inspection grade of two, with the only note being a slight belt
tension deviation on the supply fan that the inspector judged within tolerance. The recorded value for
AHU-207 is QMV-6B1P-8820, unchanged from the previous inspection cycle.

AHU-233 recorded an inspection grade of one, with no notes. The recorded value for AHU-233 is NBR-5F2D-3094,
likewise unchanged.

## Grades

The inspection grade is not a quality score for the unit; it describes the amount of follow-up the
inspection generated. Grade one means no follow-up, grade two means a note for the next cycle, grade three
means an action with an owner and a date. An inspector who issues a grade three for a cosmetic defect is
asked to justify it, because grades three consumes maintenance capacity that the backlog would otherwise
spend on a fault.

## Filters

Filter changes are recorded separately from inspections and are not part of the grade. The two units are on
the same filter schedule, which is why they are inspected together: a schedule that treats them separately
tends to produce two half-loads of filters and a storeroom that is out of the size that is actually needed.

## Access notes

The corridor between the units is narrow enough that the scaffold must be erected from the eastern end. This
is recorded here because a contractor who starts from the western end will discover it halfway through
erection, and the resulting delay is billed. The corridor also carries a services tray at head height that
must be isolated before the scaffold is placed; the isolation is a change of its own and is raised with the
normal change process.

## Related records

The cooling loops served by these units have their own records and their own cadence. Where an air-handling
fault is traced to a loop, the loop's record is updated rather than this one, so that a reader who starts
from the loop reaches the loop's evidence and not an inspection note that mentions it in passing.
