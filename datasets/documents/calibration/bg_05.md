# Retention and evidence policy

## Purpose

This policy fixes how long operational records are kept and what counts as evidence when a decision is
challenged later. It exists because the estate has twice been asked to justify a change that nobody could
reconstruct from memory.

## What is retained

Change tickets, incident timelines and their attachments are retained for seven years. Badge logs are
retained for two years, with the exception of entries linked to an incident, which inherit the incident's
retention period. Audit packs — the signed checklist, the photographs and the reviewer's note — are retained
for seven years and are stored with the ticket rather than in a shared folder.

Recorded values published in registers are retained for the life of the asset plus three years, because a
decommissioned asset can still be the subject of a warranty claim long after the physical unit is gone.

## What is not retained

Working notes, drafts and chat transcripts are not records. They are deleted after thirty days and are never
cited in a postmortem. If a working note contains the only copy of a decision, the decision is written into
the ticket; the note is not promoted to a record by renaming it.

Personal data is minimised at capture. A register entry that identifies a person is reviewed annually and
replaced with a role where the person is not material to the record.

## Legal hold

A legal hold suspends deletion for everything it names, and the suspension is applied by the records owner
within one business day of the notice. Holds are reviewed every six months; a hold that is still open after
two years requires a written justification from the requesting function, which is unusual enough that it is
noticed.

## Evidence quality

Evidence is accepted when it is contemporaneous, attributable and reproducible. A screenshot without a
timestamp is attributable in principle but not contemporaneous in practice, so the tooling stamps every
capture at the point of capture rather than at the point of upload.

Where evidence is contradictory, the reviewer records both versions and the resolution, and does not delete
the version that turned out to be wrong. The record of a mistake is worth more than a clean file.

## Review

The policy is reviewed annually by the records owner and the security lead together. The review looks at
two numbers: the share of sampled tickets that carry complete evidence, and the number of deletion
exceptions granted. Both are reported to the operations group without commentary, so that the trend is read
from the numbers rather than from the framing.
