# Capacity review — Q1 memo

The quarterly capacity review compares peak demand against provisioned headroom for each hall, and flags any
hall whose peak-to-average ratio exceeds four. Recorded values for the quarter are held in the calibration
register rather than in this memo, so that a single figure has a single home.

## Summary for the quarter

Demand grew in two halls and was flat in the rest. The growth is concentrated in the batch window between
02:00 and 05:00, when reconciliation jobs overlap with the nightly backup. Outside that window the halls run
at their usual profile, which is why an average-based view understates the pressure.

Three halls were flagged. Two were flagged on the ratio and one on absolute headroom: it can clear the ratio
test and still be one failed unit away from a hard limit, and the review treats both conditions as flags
with the same review path.

## Forecast

The forecast assumes the batch window does not widen. If the reconciliation jobs move into the working day,
the peak shifts rather than grows and the flags above become more urgent than their numbers suggest. The
forecast is revised after the mid-quarter checkpoint, and the revision is published as a new version of
this memo rather than as an appended paragraph, so a reader always knows which version they are holding.

## Actions

- Rebalance the batch schedule so that backup and reconciliation do not overlap. Owner: data platform.
- Order one spare unit for the hall flagged on headroom. Owner: procurement, with facilities confirming the
  physical space before the order is placed.
- Re-run the ratio calculation after the rebalance and publish the result with the next review.

None of the actions changes a tenancy. The review opens a discussion; it does not move a service, and it
does not authorise work on a hall. Those decisions belong to the change process and are recorded there.

## Method note

The ratio is computed from fifteen-minute samples, not hourly averages, because hourly averages hide exactly
the overlap the review is looking for. Samples with a known instrumentation fault are excluded and the
exclusion is listed in the appendix so that a future reader can reproduce the figure. Where a hall has less
than a full quarter of clean samples it is reported as provisional and carries no flag either way.
