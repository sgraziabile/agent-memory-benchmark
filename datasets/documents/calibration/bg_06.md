# Secrets rotation runbook

## Schedule

Service credentials rotate every ninety days. The schedule is published two weeks ahead and the rotation is
announced on the change channel the morning it runs, even though it needs no approval: an announcement is
how a failed rotation gets noticed by someone other than the rotation owner.

## Steps

1. Generate the new credential in the secret store. Do not paste it into a ticket, a chat message or a
   shell history; the store is the only place it exists in readable form.
2. Deploy the new credential to the first consumer and confirm one successful request before touching any
   other consumer. Rotating is a migration, not an edit.
3. Move the remaining consumers one at a time, oldest deployment first. A consumer that fails at this step
   is rolled back rather than retried, because a retry loop against a bad credential locks the account.
4. Revoke the previous credential only after every consumer has reported success for one full polling cycle.
   The overlap window is the point of the process; it is not slack to be reclaimed.
5. Record the completion in the runbook with the date, the consumers covered and anything that behaved
   unexpectedly.

## Failure handling

If a consumer cannot be migrated, the rotation is paused rather than abandoned, and the pause is recorded
with the reason and the owner of the blocking consumer. Paused rotations older than one week are escalated
to the security lead, who can either extend the old credential's life or require the consumer to be retired.

A rotation that fails after revocation is an incident, not a runbook step. Escalate immediately; the recovery
path depends on whether the old credential can be reinstated, which is a decision for the security lead and
not for the person holding the pager.

## Verification

Two checks run after every rotation. The first confirms that no consumer is still presenting the old
credential. The second confirms that the credential store and the consumers agree on the current version
identifier, which catches the case where a consumer accepted a new credential but recorded it under the old
version name.

Both checks are automated. Their results are attached to the runbook entry, so a future reader can see that
verification happened rather than reading an assertion that it did.

## Scope note

This runbook covers service credentials only. Certificates follow the lifecycle described by the issuing
authority, and the estate does not run its own certificate authority. Licence keys are handled by
procurement and are out of scope here.
