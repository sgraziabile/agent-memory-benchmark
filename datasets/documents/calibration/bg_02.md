# Access control — facility policy, section 3

## 3.1 Badge tiers

The estate recognises four badge tiers. A tier-1 badge opens the shared entrances and the meeting rooms on
the first floor. A tier-2 badge adds the operations floor and the shared workshop. A tier-3 badge adds the
plant rooms on a named list, and a tier-4 badge adds everything on that list plus the network core.

Tier changes are requested by the line manager and approved by the site security lead. Requests are
processed in the weekly access review, not on demand, unless the requester's manager is willing to page the
security lead outside hours and record the reason for the exception.

## 3.2 Escorted access

Contractors hold no standing tier. They are issued a visitor badge that expires at the end of the working
day and they are escorted at all times inside the plant rooms. The escort is a named member of staff, not a
group; an escort who is called away hands the visitor back to reception rather than leaving them seated in
a corridor.

Visitors sign a printed register on arrival and departure. The register exists because badge logs alone
cannot distinguish a visitor who left with their escort from a visitor who left alone an hour later, which
is the question the security lead asks first after any incident.

## 3.3 Keys and cabinets

Physical keys are held in the cabinet beside the north corridor entrance and signed out individually.
Cabinet keys are numbered; a missing key freezes every lock in its group until the cylinder is replaced,
which is deliberately more expensive than the alternative of keeping a spare under a desk.

## 3.4 Reviews and revocation

Access is reviewed quarterly against the current staffing list. Leavers are removed on their last working
day by the same step that closes their payroll record, so the two lists cannot drift apart. Anyone returning
from more than eight weeks of leave is re-reviewed before their badge is reactivated, because a role can
change while a badge is dormant.

## 3.5 Recording

Every entry, exit and refused attempt is recorded with badge number, door, direction and timestamp, and the
log is retained for the period set out in the retention policy. The security group samples refused attempts
weekly; a cluster of refusals at one door usually means an entitlement that was never removed rather than an
intruder, and both explanations are worth chasing.

## 3.6 Exceptions

Two standing exceptions exist. The loading bay door is opened by the third-party courier under a signature
log rather than by badge. The rooftop plant enclosure is opened by facilities only, and its door alarm is
routed to the on-call engineer rather than to the central station, so an alarm there is answered by a phone
call to the engineer on shift.
