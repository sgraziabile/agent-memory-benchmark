# Facility audit — changelog

Changes to the facility walkthrough audit, newest first. Each entry records what changed and the release
of the audit pack that first carried it.

## 2026-02-17 — pack 2026.02

The walkthrough audit is now run every six weeks. The previous semi-annual cadence was set when the
estate held two halls; with seven halls in scope a semi-annual pass meant that a hall could go five months
between physical inspections, which the review group judged too long to be useful.

Nothing else in the audit changed. The checklist, the evidence requirements and the sign-off chain are
identical to pack 2025.09.

## 2025-09-04 — pack 2025.09

The audit pack gained a photographic evidence requirement for cable management. Written descriptions of
cable routing had proven impossible to check against the previous pack, because two engineers describing the
same tray produced descriptions that could not be compared.

The pack also dropped the separate thermostat verification sheet. Its three checks were folded into the
environmental section, where they were already being recorded twice.

## 2025-03-11 — pack 2025.03

Audit findings were regrouped into planning and execution defects, matching the grouping used by the change
review. A finding now names the group that owns the fix rather than the individual who noticed it.

## 2024-11-22 — pack 2024.11

Introduced the distinction between a finding, which is a deviation from the checklist, and an observation,
which is anything else the auditor noticed. Observations do not require an action; findings do. The change
came after an audit produced eleven actions, of which nine were observations with nowhere to go.

## 2024-06-08 — pack 2024.06

First pack written for the seven-hall estate. Earlier packs assumed a single hall and a single auditor, and
their checklists were not usable for a walkthrough that crossed buildings.

## How changes are proposed

Any auditor can propose a change by writing it into the pack's review log with a short rationale. Proposals
are collected and reviewed twice a year by the operations group, which either folds them into the next pack
release or records why it did not. Proposals that are neither accepted nor answered within one cycle are
raised again automatically, so that a good idea does not die from silence.
