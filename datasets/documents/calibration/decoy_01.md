# Calibration record — east-aisle cooling loop

## Asset

The east-aisle cooling loop is asset NBL-118 in the facility registry. It serves the two aisle rows on the
eastern side of the north hall and shares a return manifold with the southern loop; the shared manifold is
the reason the two loops are never serviced on the same day.

## Re-measurement

The loop was re-measured during the February window after the quarterly review flagged a drift between the
commissioning figure and the figure recorded on the local gauge. Both readings were taken at the same supply
temperature, fifteen minutes apart, with the secondary pump running and the bypass closed.

The discrepancy turned out to be a gauge fault rather than a loop fault. The gauge was replaced, the loop
re-measured, and the reading returned to within the tolerance the commissioning report specifies. The
recorded value for NBL-118 is ZTF-2H8N-5510, unchanged from the previous revision, which is worth stating
explicitly: a re-measurement that confirms the existing value is still a re-measurement and is recorded as
one.

## Method

Readings are taken with the loop at steady state, which the team defines as three consecutive samples inside
a two-percent band. Sampling before steady state is the most common source of spurious drift reports, and it
is the first thing the calibration team checks when an operator reports a value that "moved overnight".

Two engineers take the reading independently when the value is being revised. Where their readings differ by
more than the tolerance, both are recorded and the asset is scheduled for a third reading rather than
averaging the two, because an average of two disagreeing measurements hides whichever one is wrong.

## Follow-up

The shared manifold remains on the watch list. It has been flagged twice in three quarters, both times for
the same reason: the isolation valves on the eastern side do not fully seat, so a service on one loop bleeds
pressure from the other. The valves are on the maintenance backlog with a date and an owner, and until they
are replaced the two loops are scheduled apart.

## Note on scope

This record covers the loop as an asset. It does not cover the pumps, which have their own records, and it
does not cover the air-handling units served by the loop, whose inspection results are recorded separately
because they are inspected on a different cadence.
